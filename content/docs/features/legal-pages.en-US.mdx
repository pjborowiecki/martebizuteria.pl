M'Arte has three legal documents: the terms of service (the regulamin) at `/terms-of-service`, the privacy policy at `/privacy-policy` and the returns policy at `/exchanges-and-returns`. Each one is a row in the D1 `content_page` table. Admins edit them at `/admin/content` in a rich-text editor that writes Markdown, and the storefront renders that Markdown with the same typography the editor shows.

## The table

`src/modules/content-page/content-page.schema.ts` defines one row per page:

| Column         | Holds                                                                    |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `handle`       | `terms-of-service`, `privacy-policy` or `exchanges-and-returns`, unique  |
| `titles`       | A JSON map from locale to the page title                                 |
| `descriptions` | A JSON map from locale to the meta description                           |
| `bodies`       | A JSON map from locale to the Markdown body                              |
| `revised_ats`  | A JSON map from locale to the epoch milliseconds of the last real change |
| `updated_at`   | The last save of any kind, used to detect conflicting saves              |

The handle column is an enum over `CONTENT_PAGE_HANDLES` in `content-page.constants.ts`, so a page the code does not know about cannot be stored or requested.

The migration `20261002120000_content_pages.sql` creates the table and seeds the privacy and returns pages with the text that used to live in `content/legal`. `20261005200000_terms_of_service_page.sql` adds the terms of service. Both insert with `ON CONFLICT(handle) DO NOTHING`, so running them again never overwrites text an admin has already edited.

`20261005210000_returns_and_complaints_page.sql` replaces the seeded returns text, which contradicted Polish consumer law. It updates both bodies only while each one still equals the text the first migration seeded, compared per locale with `json_extract`, so a returns page an admin has edited in either language is left alone. A page saved without changes is still rewritten. The update sets `revised_ats` and `updated_at` to 5 October 2026, 10:00 UTC, so the page shows the date of the new text and an editor opened before the migration reports a conflict instead of saving over it.

`20261010200000_privacy_policy_page.sql` replaces the seeded privacy policy in the same way, only while both bodies still equal the seeded text, and dates the new text 10 October 2026, 10:00 UTC. The seeded policy put the newsletter under legitimate interest although the form asks for consent, named no processors, said nothing about transfers to the US or how long data is kept, and described cookies the site does not set.

`20261010210000_privacy_policy_account_deletion.sql` changes one sentence of the newsletter section, which said that deleting the account does not end the subscription, to say that it ends the subscription for the account's e-mail address and removes that address from the list, as `eraseAccountData` now does. It runs one `replace` per locale, only while that body still contains the old sentence, and dates that locale's revision and `updated_at` 10 October 2026, 12:00 UTC, so a policy an admin has rewritten keeps the admin's text in that language.

The Polish documents are the authoritative ones. They cite Polish statute directly, such as the 14-day right of withdrawal, articles 7a, 38 and 38a of the Consumer Rights Act and the GDPR article for each processing purpose, and they name the data controller. Keep the English versions in step as translations, not as separate texts.

## The terms of service

The terms are the regulamin that Article 8 of the Polish Act on the Provision of Services by Electronic Means requires, and they carry the pre-contract information of Article 12 of the Consumer Rights Act that the checkout does not show itself. The checkout, the sign-up form and the newsletter form all ask the customer to accept them. Every fact in them comes from the code, so a change to one of these sources needs a matching edit to the terms in `/admin/content`:

| Section | What it states                                                                                                           | Where it comes from                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| § 1     | The seller's legal name, address, NIP, REGON, e-mail and telephone                                                       | `BUSINESS` in `src/presentation/branding/business.ts`                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| § 4     | The account, the wishlist of up to 200 products, the newsletter confirmed by e-mail and the cart kept in the browser     | Better Auth, `WISHLIST_MAX_ITEMS`, the newsletter use cases and `cart.store.ts`                                                                                                                                                                          |
| § 5     | The checkout steps, the "Zamawiam i płacę" button and a contract concluded once the paid order is accepted               | The checkout steps, `placeOrder` and `handleFulfillCheckoutSession`                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| § 6     | Card, BLIK and Przelewy24 through Stripe, paid in advance, and one discount code per order                               | `CHECKOUT_PAYMENT_METHOD_ORDER` and the discount module                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| § 7     | Delivery in Poland only by Kurier InPost, Paczkomat InPost or free collection in Bochnia from 09:00 to 17:00             | `DELIVERY_COUNTRY_CODES`, which the checkout enforces, `delivery-methods.sql` and `BUSINESS.OPENING_HOURS_*`                                                                                                                                             |
| § 7     | A fulfilment time taken from the product page, 10 business days when it has none, plus up to 4 business days with InPost | The `czas-realizacji` attribute, which the product page and the order confirmation read, the 10 days of `fallbackTime` in `pages.product` and `fulfillmentTime.fallback` in `emails.order-confirmation`, and the longest transit time the emails promise |
| § 8–10  | Withdrawal, conformity, complaints and out-of-court routes under the Consumer Rights Act                                 | The statute; the returns page, the privacy policy and the two PDF forms are linked rather than repeated                                                                                                                                                  |

The courier and locker prices are not written into the terms. They live in `delivery_method` rows, which the seed file does not keep in step with production, so the terms say the checkout shows each price before the order is placed. The terms do not link the EU online dispute resolution platform, which closed on 20 July 2025.

The newsletter section says that deleting the account ends the subscription for the account's address, which `eraseAccountData` (`src/modules/user/user.erasure.server.ts`) does. See [Account: Closing the account](/docs/features/account#closing-the-account).

The cookies are listed under their production names, with the `__Secure-` prefix Better Auth adds when `useSecureCookies` is on. Better Auth sets the session cookie for 7 days, a copy of it per signed-in account through the multi-session plugin, `state` for 5 minutes during Google sign-in, and `two_factor` for 10 minutes and `trust_device` for 30 days during two-factor authentication. A new cookie, storage key or third-party script, such as analytics, needs an entry in both languages before it ships.

## The returns page and the forms

The returns page is the practical guide to § 8 and § 9 of the terms and links to them, so the two must not disagree. It states only what the Consumer Rights Act allows:

| Topic      | What the page says                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Withdrawal | 14 days from receipt, by e-mail or by post, with or without the form; the goods go back within 14 days of withdrawing, at the customer's direct cost; liability only for diminished value                 |
| Refunds    | Within 14 days of the statement, including the cheapest standard delivery, to the same means of payment (card, BLIK or Przelewy24), which is where Stripe refunds go; no bank account number is collected |
| Exception  | Article 38(1)(3) covers only pieces made to the customer's own specification, not the sizes and finishes offered as variants                                                                              |
| Complaints | Liability for non-conformity for 2 years under articles 43a–43g, not a guarantee; repair or replacement first, then price reduction or withdrawal; the seller pays to ship the item both ways             |
| Reply      | Within 14 calendar days of receiving the complaint, after which it is deemed accepted under article 7a                                                                                                    |

The two PDFs are static files in `public/forms`, linked from the returns page and the terms in both languages, and the order confirmation attaches the withdrawal form, so keep their paths:

- `formularz_zwrotu.pdf` is the model withdrawal form from Annex 2 to the Consumer Rights Act, word for word, with the seller's name, postal address and e-mail filled in as the addressee.
- `formularz_reklamacji.pdf` is a complaint form addressed to the seller that offers the four statutory remedies and asks for no bank account, because refunds go back to the original means of payment.

Both forms are in Polish. The English returns page says so at the top and gives the English wording of the model withdrawal form from Annex I(B) to Directive 2011/83/EU as a list the customer can copy into an e-mail. The PDFs were printed from HTML with Chromium's `page.pdf()` in the Manrope and Cormorant Garamond faces from `public/fonts`, with the fonts embedded so Polish characters render in any viewer. If anything in `BUSINESS` changes, print both forms again with the new details.

## The order confirmation

Article 21 of the Consumer Rights Act requires the seller to confirm a distance contract on a durable medium, by delivery at the latest, with the pre-contract information of Article 12. The order confirmation email does this for every order, guests included, so it does not rely on the customer opening the terms on the site. It is sent from the Stripe webhook once the paid order is accepted, which is the moment § 5 of the terms says the contract is concluded, in the language of the checkout:

| Part of the email | What it states                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Where it comes from                                                                                                                                     |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Order summary     | The items, the total with VAT and delivery, the payment and delivery method, the addresses                                                                                                                                                      | The order row, the checkout and Stripe                                                                                                                  |
| Delivery time     | The longest fulfilment time the ordered products' pages give, or 10 business days for a page without one, counted from the acceptance of the order, then up to 4 business days in transit, or collection once it ends                           | `getFulfillmentTimeAttributesByVariantId`, `describeOrderFulfillmentTime`, and `fulfillmentTime` and `estimatedDelivery` in `emails.order-confirmation` |
| Withdrawal        | 14 days from taking possession, a statement by e-mail or post, the attached form, return within 14 days at the customer's direct cost, the refund rules                                                                                         | `withdrawal` in `emails.order-confirmation`, `BUSINESS.EMAIL`                                                                                           |
| Complaints        | 2-year liability for non-conformity, the remedies, where to send it, shipping paid by the seller, the 14-day answer, and the out-of-court routes of § 10: the consumer ombudsman, the Trade Inspection, consumer organisations and uokik.gov.pl | `complaints` in `emails.order-confirmation`, `BUSINESS.EMAIL`                                                                                           |
| Documents         | Links to the terms, the exchanges-and-returns page and the privacy policy                                                                                                                                                                       | `buildOrderLegalLinks` on the address the order was placed on                                                                                           |
| Seller            | The legal name, postal address, NIP, e-mail and phone                                                                                                                                                                                           | `BUSINESS` and `BUSINESS_POSTAL_ADDRESS_LINES` in `business.ts`                                                                                         |
| Attachment        | `formularz_zwrotu.pdf`, the model withdrawal form                                                                                                                                                                                               | `public/forms`, read through the `ASSETS` binding                                                                                                       |

The withdrawal and complaint texts summarise § 8 to § 10 rather than quote them, so a change to those sections of the terms needs the same change in both `emails.order-confirmation` message files. A confirmation is never sent without the form: if the Worker cannot read it, the order records the email as failed, as [Transactional email](/docs/features/email#order-confirmation-in-detail) describes.

## The storefront pages

All three routes load the page through one query and hand rendering to a shared article:

```tsx
const PrivacyPolicyPage = (): JSX.Element => <ContentPageArticle handle={CONTENT_PAGE_HANDLE.PRIVACY_POLICY} />

export const Route = createFileRoute("/_storefront/privacy-policy")({
  component: PrivacyPolicyPage,
  head: pageHead,
  loader: async ({ context }) => {
    const page = await context.queryClient.query(getContentPageQuery(CONTENT_PAGE_HANDLE.PRIVACY_POLICY, context.locale))

    return { description: page.description, title: page.title } satisfies PageMeta
  },
})
```

`getContentPage` in `src/modules/content-page/use-cases/get-content-page.ts` is a public `GET` server function. It resolves the title, description and body for the requested locale, falling back to Polish, and throws `notFound()` for an unknown handle. It also returns `revisedAt`, the revision date for that locale.

`ContentPageArticle` reads the same query with `useSuspenseQuery`, so the loader's fetch is reused rather than repeated. It renders the title as the only `<h1>`, a "Last updated" line formatted with use-intl, and the body:

```tsx
const { Markdown } = createMarkdownRenderer({ remarkPlugins: [remarkGfm, remarkHeading] })

<Markdown components={MARKDOWN_COMPONENTS}>{page.body}</Markdown>
```

`createMarkdownRenderer` comes from `fumadocs-core` and compiles Markdown at render time, not at build time. Raw HTML in a body is dropped rather than rendered. `remarkHeading` gives every heading a slug `id`, so a link such as `/exchanges-and-returns#complaints` lands on its section. The only component override is the link, which adds the reader's locale prefix to store paths and leaves files such as `/forms/formularz_zwrotu.pdf` alone.

The typography lives in `CONTENT_PROSE_CLASS` in `content-page.styles.ts`. The admin editor applies the same class to its editable area, so what an admin sees while typing is what customers see.

All three routes sit under `_storefront`, so they get the navigation, the cart availability banner and the footer.

## Editing in the admin panel

The **Content** item in the admin sidebar opens `/admin/content`, which lists every page with its last save. Each one opens `/admin/content/$handle`. The route rejects any handle outside `CONTENT_PAGE_HANDLES` with a 404.

The editor page has one tab per locale. Each tab holds three fields:

- **Title**, up to 120 characters, shown as the page heading and in the browser tab.
- **Description**, up to 300 characters with a live counter, used for search results and link previews.
- **Content**, the rich-text body.

The body field is [MDXEditor](https://mdxeditor.dev), which is built on Lexical. `src/integrations/mdxeditor/mdxeditor.plugins.ts` enables only what the storefront can show:

| Toolbar control | Markdown written                          |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Text style      | Paragraph, `##` heading, `###` subheading |
| Bold, Italic    | `**bold**`, `*italic*`                    |
| Lists           | `- item` and `1. item`                    |
| Link            | `[text](href)`                            |
| Divider         | `---`                                     |

Quotes are available through Markdown shortcuts, such as typing `> ` at the start of a line. Shift+Enter starts a new line inside a paragraph, which is how a postal address stays on separate lines. The editor writes it as a trailing backslash, and a custom export visitor keeps two consecutive line breaks as a paragraph break instead.

MDXEditor serialises through `mdast-util-to-markdown`, so text that would otherwise read as Markdown is escaped. A line that starts with `1.` or `-`, a trailing `#` in a heading and emphasis next to punctuation all survive a save unchanged. The editor tests load every seeded page in both locales into the real editor and check that they come back byte-identical.

The editor's own labels, such as the link dialog and the heading names, come from `pages.admin.content.editor.mdx` in the admin message files through `useContentEditorTranslation`. The editable area takes its accessible name from the field label.

## Saving

**Save** is enabled once something has changed. Ctrl+S or ⌘+S saves too. Leaving the page with unsaved changes asks for confirmation, and closing the tab triggers the browser's own warning.

The form is validated with `contentPageZodSchemas.formValues` before anything is sent. Every locale needs a title, a description and a body. A body may not contain an image, and every link must be an `https://`, `http://`, `mailto:` or `tel:` address or a path on this site. When a locale fails, the editor switches to that tab and marks it, and the toast lists every locale with errors.

`updateContentPage` requires the `content: ["update"]` permission, which only admins have. It saves with optimistic concurrency:

```ts
.where(and(eq(contentPage.handle, handle), eq(contentPage.updatedAt, expectedUpdatedAt)))
```

If another admin saved the page after this one opened it, nothing is written and the editor shows a conflict toast with a **Reload** action. Reload fetches the latest version and replaces the form.

A save publishes immediately. It also:

- moves the "Last updated" date only for the locales whose title or body changed, so fixing a typo in a description does not change the date;
- schedules a realtime invalidation, so open storefront and admin tabs refetch the page;
- records a `content_page_updated` entry under the Content category of the [audit log](/docs/features/audit-log).

Edits typed while a save is in flight are kept after it completes.

## Adding another page

1. Add the handle to `CONTENT_PAGE_HANDLE` and `CONTENT_PAGE_HANDLES`, and its route to `ROUTES` and `CONTENT_PAGE_PATHS`.
2. Write a migration that inserts the row with every locale's title, description and body, using `ON CONFLICT(handle) DO NOTHING`.
3. Copy one of the storefront routes and change the handle.
4. Link the page from the footer if customers should find it there.

The admin list and editor pick the new page up without changes.

## Message-only storefront pages

Two other pages in the footer's information column are not content pages and have no long-form text:

| Route    | File                    | What it renders                                                                                     |
| -------- | ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `/faq`   | `_storefront.faq.tsx`   | `t("title")`, one `t("description")` paragraph, a link home                                         |
| `/about` | `_storefront.about.tsx` | An eyebrow, a title, a lead and four paragraphs from `pages.about`, then the seller contact section |

`/about` is finished copy authored in `messages/{locale}/pages.about.json`. `/faq` is a placeholder and renders a centred "go home" link.

The about page ends with a "Contact details" section whose id is `ABOUT_CONTACT_HASH` from `src/routes.ts`, so `/about#contact` scrolls to it. It renders `SellerContactDetails` from `src/presentation/components/custom/seller-contact-details.tsx`: the legal name and postal address from `BUSINESS_POSTAL_ADDRESS_LINES` with the country, the NIP and REGON, and the e-mail address and phone number as `mailto:` and `tel:` links. Every value comes from `BUSINESS` in `src/presentation/branding/business.ts`; only the country name and the NIP and REGON labels are messages, in `components.custom.sellerContactDetails`.

## Footer links

`src/presentation/components/custom/pages/landing-page/footer/footer.tsx` links these pages:

```tsx
<FooterLink to={ROUTES.TERMS_OF_SERVICE}>{t("terms")}</FooterLink>
<FooterLink to={ROUTES.PRIVACY_POLICY}>{t("privacy")}</FooterLink>
<FooterLink to={ROUTES.EXCHANGES_AND_RETURNS}>{t("returns")}</FooterLink>
```

The footer's "Contact details" link, like the "Contact" link in the full-screen menu, goes to `ROUTES.ABOUT` with `hash={ABOUT_CONTACT_HASH}`, the contact section at the end of the about page. Below the link columns, under the "Seller and contact" heading, the footer also renders `SellerContactDetails`, so the seller's legal name, address, NIP, REGON, e-mail and phone are on every page that has the footer.

The checkout payment step also links the terms and privacy pages through `t.rich("termsAgreement", { privacy, terms })`. See [Checkout](/docs/features/checkout).
