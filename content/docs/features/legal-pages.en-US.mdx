M'Arte has three legal documents: the terms of service (the regulamin) at `/terms-of-service`, the privacy policy at `/privacy-policy` and the returns policy at `/exchanges-and-returns`. Each one is a row in the D1 `content_page` table, and so is the FAQ at `/faq`. Admins edit them at `/admin/content` in a rich-text editor that writes Markdown, and the storefront renders that Markdown with the same typography the editor shows.

## The table

`src/modules/content-page/content-page.schema.ts` defines one row per page:

| Column         | Holds                                                                          |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `handle`       | `terms-of-service`, `privacy-policy`, `exchanges-and-returns` or `faq`, unique |
| `titles`       | A JSON map from locale to the page title                                       |
| `descriptions` | A JSON map from locale to the meta description                                 |
| `bodies`       | A JSON map from locale to the Markdown body                                    |
| `revised_ats`  | A JSON map from locale to the epoch milliseconds of the last real change       |
| `updated_at`   | The last save of any kind, used to detect conflicting saves                    |

The handle column is an enum over `CONTENT_PAGE_HANDLES` in `content-page.constants.ts`, so a page the code does not know about cannot be stored or requested.

The migration `20261002120000_content_pages.sql` creates the table and seeds the privacy and returns pages with the text that used to live in `content/legal`. `20261005200000_terms_of_service_page.sql` adds the terms of service. Both insert with `ON CONFLICT(handle) DO NOTHING`, so running them again never overwrites text an admin has already edited.

`20261005210000_returns_and_complaints_page.sql` replaces the seeded returns text, which contradicted Polish consumer law. It updates both bodies only while each one still equals the text the first migration seeded, compared per locale with `json_extract`, so a returns page an admin has edited in either language is left alone. A page saved without changes is still rewritten. The update sets `revised_ats` and `updated_at` to 5 October 2026, 10:00 UTC, so the page shows the date of the new text and an editor opened before the migration reports a conflict instead of saving over it.

`20261010200000_privacy_policy_page.sql` replaces the seeded privacy policy in the same way, only while both bodies still equal the seeded text, and dates the new text 10 October 2026, 10:00 UTC. The seeded policy put the newsletter under legitimate interest although the form asks for consent, named no processors, said nothing about transfers to the US or how long data is kept, and described cookies the site does not set.

`20261010210000_privacy_policy_account_deletion.sql` changes one sentence of the newsletter section, which said that deleting the account does not end the subscription, to say that it ends the subscription for the account's e-mail address and removes that address from the list, as `eraseAccountData` now does. It runs one `replace` per locale, only while that body still contains the old sentence, and dates that locale's revision and `updated_at` 10 October 2026, 12:00 UTC, so a policy an admin has rewritten keeps the admin's text in that language.

`20261010231000_withdrawal_function_links.sql` points the returns page and § 8 of the terms at the online withdrawal function. The first step of "How to withdraw" on the returns page now starts with the form at `/withdraw-from-contract` and the "Withdraw from the contract" button on the order in the account, and keeps the e-mail and postal routes after them; the English page also says that only the PDF forms are in Polish. § 8 names the function before e-mail and post. In the privacy policy it adds withdrawing by order number to the places where the policy then said a bot check runs, and its acknowledgement to the e-mails whose per-address counters the policy describes. Like the account deletion migration it runs one `replace` per page and language, only while that body still contains the old sentence, and dates each changed language and `updated_at` 10 October 2026, 12:00 UTC.

`20261012100000_privacy_policy_without_bot_check.sql` takes the bot check out of the privacy policy, because the shop no longer runs one. For each language it runs one `UPDATE` with three nested `replace` calls, only while that body still names the check. They remove the last bullet under "Security and abuse prevention", about the result of the check and the one-time token sent to Cloudflare with the IP address, so the list now ends with the bullet about the IP address kept while a payment is open, closed by a full stop; the check from Cloudflare's entry among the recipients, which now ends with "caching and protection against attacks."; and the section about the check's frame under "Cookies and other data stored in your browser", so the Stripe section now comes right before the closing paragraph about deleting cookies. It dates that language's revision and `updated_at` 12 October 2026, 12:00 UTC, so a policy an admin has rewritten keeps the admin's text and date in that language.

The Polish documents are the authoritative ones. They cite Polish statute directly, such as the 14-day right of withdrawal, articles 7a, 38 and 38a of the Consumer Rights Act and the GDPR article for each processing purpose, and they name the data controller. Keep the English versions in step as translations, not as separate texts.

## The terms of service

The terms are the regulamin that Article 8 of the Polish Act on the Provision of Services by Electronic Means requires, and they carry the pre-contract information of Article 12 of the Consumer Rights Act that the checkout does not show itself. The checkout, the sign-up form and the newsletter form all ask the customer to accept them. Every fact in them comes from the code, so a change to one of these sources needs a matching edit to the terms in `/admin/content`:

| § 4     | The account, the wishlist of up to 200 products, the newsletter confirmed by e-mail and the cart kept in the browser     | Better Auth, `WISHLIST_MAX_ITEMS`, the newsletter use cases and `cart.store.ts`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| § 5     | The checkout steps, the "Zamawiam i płacę" button and a contract concluded once the paid order is accepted               | The checkout steps, `placeOrder` and `handleFulfillCheckoutSession`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |

| § 7     | Delivery in Poland only by Kurier InPost, Paczkomat InPost or free collection in Bochnia from 09:00 to 17:00             | `DELIVERY_COUNTRY_CODES`, which the checkout enforces, the delivery methods offered under Settings → Delivery, and the pickup street and city from Settings → Store details and the hours in `deliverySubsteps.inStoreText`, both written in words here                                                                    |

§ 1 needs no edit when the seller's details change: it names them through store detail placeholders, which show what Settings → Store details holds (see [Store details in the pages](#store-details-in-the-pages)). The pickup line of § 7 writes the street and city in words, so a move of the workshop still needs an edit there.

The courier and locker prices are not written into the terms. They live in `delivery_method` rows, which the owner changes under Settings → Delivery together with the free delivery threshold, so the terms say the checkout shows each price before the order is placed. A method with another carrier, or a pickup that is no longer free, is a change to § 7 as well (see [Shipping and delivery](/docs/features/shipping-and-delivery#managing-delivery-methods)). The terms do not link the EU online dispute resolution platform, which closed on 20 July 2025.

## The returns page and the forms

The returns page is the practical guide to § 8 and § 9 of the terms and links to them, so the two must not disagree. It states only what the Consumer Rights Act allows:

| Topic      | What the page says                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Withdrawal | 14 days from receipt, with the online withdrawal function, by e-mail or by post, with or without the form; the goods go back within 14 days of withdrawing, at the customer's direct cost; liability only for diminished value |
| Refunds    | Within 14 days of the statement, including the cheapest standard delivery, to the same means of payment (card, BLIK or Przelewy24), which is where Stripe refunds go; no bank account number is collected                      |
| Exception  | Article 38(1)(3) covers only pieces made to the customer's own specification, not the sizes and finishes offered as variants                                                                                                   |
| Complaints | Liability for non-conformity for 2 years under articles 43a–43g, not a guarantee; repair or replacement first, then price reduction or withdrawal; the seller pays to ship the item both ways                                  |
| Reply      | Within 14 calendar days of receiving the complaint, after which it is deemed accepted under article 7a                                                                                                                         |

The two forms are linked from the returns page, the terms and the FAQ in both languages, and the order confirmation attaches the withdrawal form, so keep their addresses:

- `/forms/formularz_zwrotu.pdf` is the model withdrawal form from Annex 2 to the Consumer Rights Act, word for word, addressed to the seller's legal name, street, postal code, city and e-mail from Settings → Store details.
- `/forms/formularz_reklamacji.pdf` is a complaint form that names the seller with the same details, offers the four statutory remedies and asks for no bank account, because refunds go back to the original means of payment.

Both forms are in Polish. The English returns page says so at the top and gives the English wording of the model withdrawal form from Annex I(B) to Directive 2011/83/EU as a list the customer can copy into an e-mail. The Worker makes both forms from the stored details each time one is opened or attached, so a save under Settings → Store details reaches them together with the legal pages and the e-mails (see [How the forms are made](#how-the-forms-are-made)).

The home page states the same 2 years once, among the silver section's figures in `silver-premium-section.tsx`: `stats.liability` in `pages.landing` gives "2 years" over "Statutory liability for lack of conformity" ("2 lata" over "Ustawowa odpowiedzialność za brak zgodności z umową"). The shop gives no guarantee, so no storefront text may call the 2 years a guarantee or a warranty. A guarantee statement can be made in an advertisement (article 577 § 1 of the Civil Code), so such a claim would bind the seller to a guarantee whose terms were never published. The label also says the liability is statutory, because presenting a right consumers have by law as a distinctive feature of the offer is a misleading practice under article 7(10) of the Act on Counteracting Unfair Market Practices.

### How the forms are made

`WITHDRAWAL_FORM` in `src/presentation/pdf/withdrawal-form.ts` and `COMPLAINT_FORM` in `complaint-form.ts` hold each form's file name and a `render(seller, fonts)` that returns the PDF. A template lists the form's blocks in order: the line above the title, the title, the lead, the dashed statements with their writing lines, the labelled fields, the remedies with their tick boxes, the signature lines and the grey notice, with the seller's details written into the addressee, the seller block and the notice. `renderFormDocument` in `src/presentation/pdf/form-document.ts` lays them out on one A4 page with `pdf-lib`: the M'Arte wordmark and `MARTEBIZUTERIA.PL` over a rule, Manrope 400 and 600 for the text and Cormorant Garamond 600 for the wordmark and the title, in the sizes, colours, spacing and line breaks of the PDFs the shop printed from HTML with Chromium before. Words wrap at the column, so a longer name, address or e-mail takes more lines, and a long name, street, city and e-mail together still fit the page. The forms name the seller as the printed ones did, with the legal name, street, postal code, city and e-mail, and without the country, NIP, REGON or phone. The PDF's metadata carries the title, `Formularz odstąpienia od umowy – M'Arte` or `Formularz reklamacji – M'Arte`, the language `pl`, and the time the details were last saved as its creation and modification date.

The fonts are TrueType files in `public/fonts/forms`, read through the `ASSETS` binding by `loadFormFonts(origin)` in `src/modules/content-page/content-page.forms.server.ts` and embedded as subsets of the glyphs a form uses, so a form weighs about 15 KB. [Fonts and branding](/docs/ui/fonts-and-branding#fonts-in-the-pdf-forms) explains why they are not the WOFF2 files the site uses and where they come from.

`src/routes/forms.formularz_zwrotu[.]pdf.ts` and `forms.formularz_reklamacji[.]pdf.ts` are server routes at the addresses the static files had. Their `GET` handler imports `respondWithForm` and the template when it runs, so `pdf-lib` and fontkit, about 2 MB of the Worker, are loaded only when a form is made. `respondWithForm` reads the seller details and answers with:

| Header                | Value                                                                   |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `Content-Type`        | `application/pdf`                                                       |
| `Content-Disposition` | `inline; filename="formularz_zwrotu.pdf"` or `formularz_reklamacji.pdf` |
| `Cache-Control`       | `public, max-age=300, must-revalidate`                                  |
| `ETag`                | The time the seller details were last saved, in milliseconds, in quotes |

A browser that sends the `ETag` back in `If-None-Match`, as it is, as a weak tag or in a list, or sends `*`, gets `304 Not Modified`, and the form is not made again. A save changes the `ETag`, so a reader sees the new details at the latest five minutes after it. The `ETag` follows the details only: a deploy that changes a form's wording or layout reaches a browser that already holds the form once the details are saved again or the browser drops its copy.

`/forms` is among the paths `shouldIgnorePath` leaves out of locale handling, like `/api` and `/assets`, so opening a form from an English page does not switch the shop's language cookie to Polish, and `/en-US/forms/…` redirects to `/forms/…`. The Worker gives the forms the same security headers as every response it answers (see [Security model](/docs/architecture/security-model#response-headers)).

## The withdrawal function

Since 19 June 2026 Article 11a of the Consumer Rights Directive, added by Directive (EU) 2023/2673, requires a shop that sells online to let consumers withdraw online: a clearly labelled function, available for the whole withdrawal period, followed by a confirmation step, and an acknowledgement of each statement on a durable medium with its content and the date and time it was made. The shop offers it in two places:

| Where                                                                                                            | For                     | How the order is found                                                                                                 |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The order's page in the account, `/account/orders/$id`, under "Withdrawal from the contract"                     | A signed-in customer    | `withdrawFromCustomerOrder` only finds an order whose `user_id` is the session's user                                  |
| `/withdraw-from-contract`, linked from the footer, the returns page, § 8 of the terms and the order confirmation | Anyone, guests included | `withdrawFromGuestOrder` matches the order number and the e-mail address the order was placed with, in any letter case |

Both start from a "Withdraw from the contract" ("Odstąp od umowy") button or heading and end with "Confirm withdrawal" ("Potwierdź odstąpienie od umowy"). They ask for the consumer's name, which the account fills in from the account, whether the statement covers the whole order or only some products, and a reason the form marks as optional. In the account the products are ticked from the order's lines, and the server writes them out as `title — variant × quantity`; on the public page the guest describes them in a text field, because that page never shows an order's contents to someone who only knows its number and e-mail address. The order confirmation links the public page with `?order=<number>`, so the order number is already filled in.

`recordOrderWithdrawal` in `src/modules/order-withdrawal/order-withdrawal.record.server.ts` records the statement from either server function, and `sendOrderWithdrawalEmails` beside it sends its e-mails:

1. It answers `closed` unless the order is `processing` or `completed` and either has no `delivered_at` yet or is still within its withdrawal period. The period runs to the end of the fourteenth day after the day the order was marked delivered, in Polish time, and when that day is a Saturday, a Sunday or a Polish public holiday, to the end of the next working day, as Articles 111 § 2 and 115 of the Civil Code count it. `polishDeadlineDay` in `src/modules/_core/utils/polish-deadline.ts` works out that last day from the delivery date in `DEFAULT_TIMEZONE_CODE`, the shop's zone the order numbers and the admin dashboard also count in; it takes Easter Monday and Corpus Christi from the date of Easter and counts Christmas Eve among the holidays. A cancelled or refunded order has no contract left to withdraw from.
2. It answers `alreadyWithdrawn` once a statement covers the whole order. Statements for some products can follow one another.
3. It inserts an `order_withdrawal` row (`20261010230000_order_withdrawals.sql`) with the name, the order's e-mail address, the products or `NULL` for the whole order, the reason, `scope` and `created_at`, publishes the admin orders invalidation, so an open admin order page shows it, and records `order.withdrawal_received` in the audit log, credited to the consumer who signed it (see [Audit log](/docs/features/audit-log)).
4. `sendOrderWithdrawalEmails` e-mails the consumer the acknowledgement (`received` in `emails.order-withdrawal`) in the language of the request, to the order's e-mail address, and the shop the notice (`notice`) in Polish, to `RESEND_EMAIL_FROM`, with a link to the order in the admin, and waits for both. Both repeat the statement: the declaration, the products, the name, the date and time it arrived in Polish time to the second, the address and the reason. Each send is recorded on the order's timeline as "Withdrawal acknowledgement" or "Withdrawal notice". Both go through `deliverOrderEmail` with an idempotency key of their own, so the wait covers the first attempt only: one Resend refuses is tried twice more in the background, and one still refused after that alerts `RESEND_EMAIL_FROM` (see [E-mail: Delivery and retries](/docs/features/email#delivery-and-retries)).

In the account, `withdrawFromCustomerOrder` waits for the e-mails and answers whether the acknowledgement went out. If it did not, the statement still stands, and the confirmation says so and gives the shop's e-mail address. An order that belongs to someone else answers `notFound`.

The public page answers every statement alike: `withdrawFromGuestOrder` returns nothing, so the page never tells a visitor whether an order number and e-mail address match an order, or whether that order can still be withdrawn from. It thanks the visitor, says that the acknowledgement goes to that address if they match an order that can still be withdrawn from, and asks them to check both or write to the shop when nothing arrives. A recorded statement's e-mails go out in the background, and only when `admitPublicEmail` admits a `withdrawal` e-mail to the order's address: three an hour per address, within the 50 e-mails a day visitors can make the shop send (see [Resend: Emails visitors can request](/docs/integrations/resend#emails-visitors-can-request)). When it refuses, the statement is still recorded and the admin order page shows it, but neither e-mail goes out. Both server functions are limited to `RATE_LIMITS.SENSITIVE`, three requests a minute per IP address.

The account order page lists the statements already sent and drops the button once the whole order is withdrawn or the period ends, through `getCustomerOrderWithdrawal`, which also only reads the customer's own order. The admin order page shows every statement in a "Withdrawal from the contract" card above the fulfilment tracker: what it covers, when it arrived, the name and address, and the reason. Recording a statement changes nothing else on the order; refunding it and putting the pieces back in stock remain the admin's actions.

The FAQ gives the shop's e-mail address and phone number through store detail placeholders, and writes the pickup street and city in words, like § 7. ## The storefront pages

All four routes load the page through one query and hand rendering to a shared article:

```tsx
const PrivacyPolicyPage = (): JSX.Element => <ContentPageArticle handle={CONTENT_PAGE_HANDLE.PRIVACY_POLICY} />

export const Route = createFileRoute("/_storefront/privacy-policy")({
  component: PrivacyPolicyPage,
  head: pageHead,
  loader: async ({ context }) => {
    const page = await context.queryClient.query(getContentPageQuery(CONTENT_PAGE_HANDLE.PRIVACY_POLICY, context.locale))

    return { description: page.description, title: page.title } satisfies PageMeta
  },
})
```

`getContentPage` in `src/modules/content-page/use-cases/get-content-page.ts` is a public `GET` server function. It resolves the title, description and body for the requested locale, falling back to Polish, and throws `notFound()` for an unknown handle. It also returns `revisedAt`, the revision date for that locale.

`ContentPageArticle` reads the same query with `useSuspenseQuery`, so the loader's fetch is reused rather than repeated, and reads the seller's details from `getSellerDetailsQuery()`, which the `_storefront` layout prefetches. It renders the title as the only `<h1>`, a "Last updated" line formatted with use-intl, and the body:

```tsx
const { Markdown } = useMemo(
  () => createMarkdownRenderer({ remarkPlugins: [remarkGfm, remarkHeading, remarkDirective, [remarkSellerDetails, { locale, seller }]] }),
  [locale, seller],
)

<Markdown components={MARKDOWN_COMPONENTS}>{page.body}</Markdown>
```

`createMarkdownRenderer` comes from `fumadocs-core` and compiles Markdown at render time, not at build time. `remarkDirective` and `remarkSellerDetails` write out the store details (see [Store details in the pages](#store-details-in-the-pages)). Raw HTML in a body is dropped rather than rendered. `remarkHeading` gives every heading a slug `id`, so a link such as `/exchanges-and-returns#complaints` lands on its section. The only component override is the link, which adds the reader's locale prefix to store paths and leaves files such as `/forms/formularz_zwrotu.pdf` alone.

The typography lives in `CONTENT_PROSE_CLASS` in `content-page.styles.ts`. The admin editor applies the same class to its editable area, so what an admin sees while typing is what customers see.

All four routes sit under `_storefront`, so they get the navigation, the cart availability banner and the footer.

## Editing in the admin panel

The **Content** item in the admin sidebar opens `/admin/content`, which lists every page with its last save. Each one opens `/admin/content/$handle`. The route rejects any handle outside `CONTENT_PAGE_HANDLES` with a 404.

The editor page has one tab per locale. Each tab holds three fields:

- **Title**, up to 120 characters, shown as the page heading and in the browser tab.
- **Description**, up to 300 characters with a live counter, used for search results and link previews.
- **Content**, the rich-text body.

The body field is [MDXEditor](https://mdxeditor.dev), which is built on Lexical. `src/integrations/mdxeditor/mdxeditor.plugins.ts` enables only what the storefront can show:

| Toolbar control     | Markdown written                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Text style          | Paragraph, `##` heading, `###` subheading                                                                                         |
| Bold, Italic        | `**bold**`, `*italic*`                                                                                                            |
| Lists               | `- item` and `1. item`                                                                                                            |
| Link                | `[text](href)`                                                                                                                    |
| Divider             | `---`                                                                                                                             |
| Insert store detail | `:seller[nip]` and the other store details, on content pages only (see [Store details in the pages](#store-details-in-the-pages)) |

Quotes are available through Markdown shortcuts, such as typing `> ` at the start of a line. Shift+Enter starts a new line inside a paragraph, which is how a postal address stays on separate lines. The editor writes it as a trailing backslash, and a custom export visitor keeps two consecutive line breaks as a paragraph break instead.

MDXEditor serialises through `mdast-util-to-markdown`, so text that would otherwise read as Markdown is escaped. A line that starts with `1.` or `-`, a trailing `#` in a heading and emphasis next to punctuation all survive a save unchanged. The editor tests load every seeded page in both locales, with the store detail placeholders the migration put in, into the real editor and check that they come back byte-identical.

The editor's own labels, such as the link dialog and the heading names, come from `pages.admin.content.editor.mdx` in the admin message files through `useContentEditorTranslation`. The editable area takes its accessible name from the field label.

## Saving

**Save** is enabled once something has changed. Ctrl+S or ⌘+S saves too. Leaving the page with unsaved changes asks for confirmation, and closing the tab triggers the browser's own warning.

The form is validated with `contentPageZodSchemas.formValues` before anything is sent. Every locale needs a title, a description and a body. A body may not contain an image, every link must be an `https://`, `http://`, `mailto:` or `tel:` address or a path on this site, and every store detail must name one of the eleven details. When a locale fails, the editor switches to that tab and marks it, and the toast lists every locale with errors.

`updateContentPage` requires the `content: ["update"]` permission, which only admins have. It saves with optimistic concurrency:

```ts
.where(and(eq(contentPage.handle, handle), eq(contentPage.updatedAt, expectedUpdatedAt)))
```

If another admin saved the page after this one opened it, nothing is written and the editor shows a conflict toast with a **Reload** action. Reload fetches the latest version and replaces the form.

A save publishes immediately. It also:

- moves the "Last updated" date only for the locales whose title or body changed, so fixing a typo in a description does not change the date;
- schedules a realtime invalidation, so open storefront and admin tabs refetch the page;
- records a `content_page_updated` entry under the Content category of the [audit log](/docs/features/audit-log).

Edits typed while a save is in flight are kept after it completes.

## Store details in the pages

The terms, the privacy policy, the returns page and the FAQ name the seller through store details rather than typed-in values, so a change saved under Settings → Store details reaches every page and language at once (see [Admin settings](/docs/features/admin-settings#store-details)).

A store detail is a Markdown text directive, `:seller[<name>]`. `SELLER_PLACEHOLDERS` in `src/modules/content-page/content-page.seller-placeholders.ts` lists the eleven:

| Placeholder           | Shows                                     |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| `:seller[legalName]`  | The legal name                            |
| `:seller[street]`     | The street and number                     |
| `:seller[postalCode]` | The postal code                           |
| `:seller[city]`       | The city                                  |
| `:seller[country]`    | The country's name in the page's language |
| `:seller[nip]`        | The NIP                                   |
| `:seller[regon]`      | The REGON                                 |
| `:seller[email]`      | The contact e-mail                        |
| `:seller[emailLink]`  | The contact e-mail as a `mailto:` link    |
| `:seller[phone]`      | The contact phone                         |
| `:seller[phoneLink]`  | The contact phone as a `tel:` link        |

### In the editor

`ContentPageFields` passes `withSellerDetails` to `ContentEditor`, which then adds MDXEditor's `directivesPlugin` with `sellerDetailDirectivesPlugin` and, after a separator at the end of the toolbar, an **Insert store detail** dropdown (`InsertSellerDetail`); the newsletter composer leaves both out. The dropdown lists the details by name, such as **Legal name**, **NIP** or **Email (link)**, and places the chosen one where the caret is. In the text each placeholder is a chip named after its detail (`SellerDetailChip` in `src/presentation/components/custom/pages/admin/content/editor/seller-detail-directive.tsx`), which cannot be edited letter by letter, and a placeholder that names a detail that does not exist shows as a red **Unknown store detail** chip. A store detail cannot be bold or italic. The body's hint says that store details inserted from the toolbar always show what Settings › Store details holds.

The editor escapes a colon typed before a letter (`Ref\:ABC`), so typing never creates a store detail, and it escapes the colon of a `mailto:` link (`mailto\:…`). `hasOnlyMarkdownLinksMatching` in `content-page.validation.utils.ts` therefore checks each link address after undoing Markdown backslash escapes, the way the storefront reads it. `hasOnlyPublishableMarkdown` refuses a body with a `:seller[…]` that names no known detail and checks the links with the placeholders taken out; the editor reports a refused body with **The page contains an image, a link address or a store detail that cannot be published.**

### On the storefront

`ContentPageArticle` builds the Markdown renderer with `remarkDirective` from `remark-directive` and `remarkSellerDetails` (`src/presentation/components/custom/pages/content-page/remark-seller-details.ts`), given the page's locale and the details from `getSellerDetailsQuery()`. Each known placeholder becomes the stored value: `emailLink` a `mailto:` link, `phoneLink` a `tel:` link with the number's digits and plus sign, and `country` the country's name in the page's language. Any other directive, such as an unknown detail or text that only looks like one, is shown exactly as written.

### The last updated date

Saving the seller's details moves the "Last updated" date of every page and language whose body shows a detail that changed, in the same D1 batch as the details. `findLocalesShowingSellerFields` matches each placeholder to the field it shows (`emailLink` counts as the e-mail, `phoneLink` as the phone and `country` as the country code), and the page's `revised_ats` gets the save time in those languages through `json_set`. A refused save dates nothing, and the content page invalidation refreshes the dated pages in open tabs. Changing only the country, for example, dates only the English pages, because only their address blocks write the country out. Dating a page also moves its `updated_at`, so an editor already open on that page reports a conflict on its next save and asks to reload.

### The placeholder migration

`20261016161000_seller_placeholders.sql` replaced the seller's values in the seeded terms, privacy policy, returns page and FAQ, in both languages: the e-mail links with `:seller[emailLink]`, the phone links with `:seller[phoneLink]`, the legal name with `:seller[legalName]`, the street in the address blocks and in the "To:" line of the English model withdrawal form with `:seller[street]`, `32-700 Bochnia` with `:seller[postalCode] :seller[city]`, the "Poland" line of the English address blocks with `:seller[country]`, and the NIP and REGON with `:seller[nip]` and `:seller[regon]`. It touches only rows whose bodies still contain one of those values, and `replace()` changes only exact text, so a page the owner rewrote keeps its wording. It leaves `revised_ats` and `updated_at` alone, because the pages read exactly as before.

Two things stay in words: the pickup sentences in § 7 of the terms and in the FAQ ("przy ul. Wąskiej 11 w Bochni", "at ul. Wąska 11 in Bochnia"), because Polish declines the street and the city there, and the opening hours, 09:00–17:00. Moving the workshop or changing its hours still needs an edit of those sentences in `/admin/content`, and new hours need an edit of `deliverySubsteps.inStoreText` in both message files as well.

## Adding another page

1. Add the handle to `CONTENT_PAGE_HANDLE` and `CONTENT_PAGE_HANDLES`, and its route to `ROUTES` and `CONTENT_PAGE_PATHS`.
2. Write a migration that inserts the row with every locale's title, description and body, using `ON CONFLICT(handle) DO NOTHING`.
3. Copy one of the storefront routes and change the handle.
4. Link the page from the footer if customers should find it there.

The admin list and editor pick the new page up without changes.

## Manufacturer on product pages

Article 19 of the EU General Product Safety Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2023/988) requires every online offer to name the product's manufacturer with a postal and an electronic address. The shop makes the pieces it sells, so every product page ends its details with a "Manufacturer" section that names the seller: the legal name, postal address and country, and the contact e-mail as a `mailto:` link, all from Settings → Store details, labelled by messages in `pages.product.heroSection.details.manufacturer`. A piece made by someone else would need that maker's own details, which the catalog has no field for. See [Product page](/docs/features/product-page#specifications-and-details).
